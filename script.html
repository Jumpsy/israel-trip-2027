Ten Days in Israel

About 5 minutes. One section per slide in the deck.

1. Cover

I’ve planned our trip to Israel: ten days in March, from Saturday the 13th to Monday the 22nd, flying from Toronto. It’s beaches, the Galilee, Shabbat in Jerusalem and a float in the Dead Sea, and I kept it as cheap as I could without making it boring.

2. The trip at a glance

It’s one loop with four stops. Three nights in Tel Aviv to get over the jet lag, two in the Galilee, three in Jerusalem over Shabbat, and a last night back near the airport. All in, it comes to about 7,660 Canadian dollars for both of us, around 3,830 each. We rent one car for the second half.

3. Flights

The best value is LOT Polish Airlines through Warsaw. We leave Friday night the 12th at 10:35, land Saturday at 7:45 in the evening, and fly home Monday the 22nd in the morning. It’s about 1,850 a person. Ethiopian is about 270 cheaper but adds about six hours each way. Air Canada’s nonstop is the most comfortable, but it’s about 500 more.

4. Every fare

I didn’t just grab the first price. These are all 14 fares Google Flights showed for our dates, cheapest first. The ones under LOT aren’t worth it: the 1,379 one is four separate tickets on budget airlines over 33 hours, and the cheaper LOT fares add an eight-hour layover. LOT is the cheapest option that’s actually a normal trip.

5. Other weeks

I also checked three other weeks in March. If we left two days earlier, March 10 to 20, Air France via Paris is 1,688 and a bit faster, so that’s our backup plan. Late January is the cheapest window overall, but it doesn’t fit March break.

6. Hotels

Every hotel is rated 4.6 or higher. The Saul in Tel Aviv is 4.8 for 191 a night. Olea in Rosh Pinna, up north, is 4.8 for 248. In Jerusalem, Prima Royale has a 4.6 from 3,500 reviews and a kosher breakfast. The last night is at Abraham in Tel Aviv: 149, breakfast included, and 20 minutes from the airport.

7. Backup hotels

If any of those sell out, every stop has backups that are just as well rated, like the Ink Hotel in Tel Aviv, The Way Inn in Tzfat, and Leonardo in Jerusalem, which has a pool. I left out any hotel that’s adults-only.

8. Tel Aviv

We land Saturday night, grab a taxi and get a late dinner on Dizengoff. Sunday is Old Jaffa: the flea market, hummus at Abu Hassan, then the beach walk to sunset at Frishman Beach. Monday we rent city bikes up the coast to the Port, eat sabich in the Carmel Market, and finish with the street art in Florentin. Most of it is free.

9. Galilee

Tuesday we pick up the car and drive north past the Roman harbour at Caesarea. Wednesday is my favourite day: we rent a golf cart at Agamon Hula and drive around a lake full of thousands of migrating cranes, then walk the artists’ quarter in Tzfat. Thursday we hike Mount Arbel at sunrise over the Sea of Galilee.

10. Jerusalem

Thursday afternoon we drive down to Jerusalem for Yad Vashem, and that night Machane Yehuda turns into a street party. Friday is the Old City: the ramparts, the Jewish Quarter, the Kotel Tunnels, then Shabbat at the Kotel. Saturday is slow: Yemin Moshe, the windmill, the Dead Sea Scrolls at the Israel Museum, and Ben Yehuda after dark.

11. Dead Sea and home

Sunday is the big desert day. We leave at 6:30, take the cable car up Masada, see the ibex at Ein Gedi and float in the Dead Sea at the free beach. Then we drive back to Jaffa for a farewell dinner. Every flight home leaves in the morning, so sleeping near the airport makes Monday easy.

12. Optional desert night

If we want more adventure, we could swap Saturday night in Jerusalem for a Bedouin-style desert village near Masada. It’s 120 a night, so it saves about 137 overall, and we’d climb Masada at sunrise. The catch is that we’d miss Saturday night in Jerusalem.

13. Food

The best food here is also the cheapest: hummus in Jaffa, sabich, Druze pita up north, stews at Azura in the Jerusalem market, and chocolate rugelach from Marzipan. I budgeted about 85 a day for both of us.

14. Budget

Here’s where the money goes. Flights are almost half at 3,700. Hotels are about 2,000, and the car, food and activities make up the rest, for 7,660 total. Flying Ethiopian and staying at Abraham the whole time in Tel Aviv would bring it close to 7,000.

15. Before we book

This is the serious part. Canada’s travel advisory currently says to avoid non-essential travel to Israel, so we check it again right before paying, book refundable rates and get insurance that covers Israel. We also need the ETA-IL entry permit, should register with ROCA, and should install the Home Front Command app. And we never take Route 90 through the West Bank; we go around through Arad.

16. See you in March

So that’s the plan: ten days, four places, one car, about 7,660 for both of us. If we like it, the first step is booking the flights, because fares change every day. [Pause for questions.]
